How to Look Good on Video Calls in a Small Room

People buy a better webcam to fix their video calls, and the calls look almost the same afterwards. That is because the two things actually wrong are rarely the camera.

They are where the light is coming from and how high the camera sits. Both are free or cheap to fix, and neither depends on the room being large.

Fix the light direction before anything else

If there is a window or a lamp behind you, the camera exposes for the bright background and turns you into a silhouette. This is the single most common video call problem, and buying a better camera makes it worse, not better — a better sensor exposes the bright thing more accurately.

The rule: the main light source should be in front of you, roughly behind the camera.

In practice that means turning the desk so the window is in front of you rather than behind. If the desk cannot move, close the curtain during calls and light yourself from the front instead.

Get the camera to eye level

A laptop on a desk points its camera up at you from below. That angle is unflattering on everyone and it also means you appear to be looking down for the entire call, because you are looking at the screen rather than the lens.

Make the background boring on purpose

In a small room the background is close behind you and therefore in focus, which makes clutter more visible than it would be in a larger space.

You do not need a designed background. You need a calm one:

  • A plain wall section is fine and always works
  • One or two objects at different heights read as intentional
  • Anything actively in use — a laundry pile, a full shelf — reads as clutter

Avoid virtual backgrounds where you can. The edge detection fails around hair and hands, and the flicker is more distracting than a real room ever is.

Sound matters more than picture

People tolerate mediocre video and stop listening to bad audio.

The fix is distance: get the microphone closer to your mouth than the room's noise is. A headset with a boom mic does this and costs less than any camera upgrade.

Tidy just the frame

In a small room you do not need the whole room tidy. You need the visible cone behind you tidy, which is much less work.

Join a call, look at your own preview, and note what is actually in shot. Usually it is a surprisingly small area — and cables are usually the worst of it.

The checklist before an important call

  1. Light in front of you, not behind
  2. Camera at eye level
  3. Background calm, two or three deliberate objects
  4. Headset with the mic near your mouth
  5. Look at the preview once before joining

Four of the five are free. The camera you already have is almost certainly good enough.
