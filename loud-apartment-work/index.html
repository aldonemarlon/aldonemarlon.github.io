How to Work From Home in a Loud Apartment

Noise is the home office problem with the least useful advice attached to it. Most of what you find assumes you can modify the building — acoustic panels screwed to walls, sealed windows, a door you are allowed to replace.

In a rental you can do none of that. Here is what actually works, ordered by how much it helps relative to what it costs.

First: work out where the sound is getting in

This takes two minutes and changes what you should buy.

Stand in the room with the noise happening and move slowly around it. Sound entering through a door gap gets noticeably louder as you approach the door. Sound coming through a window does the same at the window. Sound through the wall or ceiling stays roughly even across the room.

That last case — even everywhere — is the one that cannot be fixed by sealing anything, and it is the case where you skip straight to headphones.

The door gap is the cheapest real win

In most apartments, more sound comes through the gap around the door than through the entire wall it sits in. A door is thin, and the gap under it is an open hole.

Adhesive door seal strip

Most sound entering a room comes through the gap under and around the door, not through the walls. Sealing that gap is the cheapest meaningful noise reduction available, and it peels off at move-out.

Adhesive seal strips around the frame plus a draft blocker along the bottom is the highest-value noise intervention available in a rental. It costs very little, it is reversible, and on interior noise — household sounds, hallway traffic — the difference is immediate.

Remove it at move-out with a hairdryer and slow pulling, same as any adhesive.

Soft surfaces, because the room amplifies what gets in

An empty room with hard floors makes every sound worse by reflecting it repeatedly. This is why the same noise is more intolerable in a bare apartment than a furnished one.

The fix is anything soft, in roughly this order of usefulness: a rug, curtains (heavy and covering more than the window), fabric furniture, wall hangings.

Curtains are the underrated one. Floor-length and wider than the window, they treat both the window as a noise entry point and the room's reflectivity at the same time.

Headphones, for everything else

Noise-cancelling headphones

The only fix that works on noise you do not control — traffic, neighbours, building work. Over-ear cancels low-frequency rumble far better than earbuds, and rumble is what makes an apartment hard to concentrate in.

For noise you genuinely cannot control — traffic, construction, a neighbour's routine — active noise cancelling is the only thing that works, and it works very well on exactly the sounds that are hardest to block physically.

Over-ear rather than in-ear. Low-frequency rumble is what makes an apartment tiring to concentrate in, and over-ear cancels it far better. In-ear models handle voices adequately and rumble poorly.

Comfort over cancellation depth. You will wear these for hours. A slightly less effective pair you can wear all afternoon beats a stronger pair you take off after ninety minutes.

Position the desk away from the noise

Free, and skipped constantly.

If the noise comes from one wall, put the desk against the opposite one. Distance helps more than people expect, and putting a bookshelf or wardrobe against the shared wall puts mass between you and the source.

Freestanding narrow shelf unit

Vertical storage that leans on the floor instead of the wall, so nothing gets drilled and nothing needs patching at move-out. Fits gaps under 12 inches wide.

A loaded shelf unit against a noisy wall does real acoustic work — mass is what blocks sound, and books are mass. It is also storage, which the room needed anyway.

Manage calls separately

Noise you can hear is one problem. Noise your microphone picks up is another, and it has a different solution: a headset with a boom mic close to your mouth, so the microphone gain stays low and the room stays out of the call.

Turning on your software's noise suppression helps too, and costs nothing.

In order, if you are spending gradually

  1. Locate the entry point — free, and decides everything after
  2. Seal the door — cheapest meaningful reduction
  3. Rug and heavy curtains — treats the room's own amplification
  4. Move the desk — free
  5. Noise-cancelling headphones — for everything the first four cannot reach

Most people start at step five. It works, but the first four are cheaper and they keep working when the headphones are charging.
