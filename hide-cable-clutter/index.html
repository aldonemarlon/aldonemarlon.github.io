Cable mess is responsible for more of the "this room feels messy" feeling than almost anything else, and it is out of proportion to how much space it takes up.

The reason is position. Cables sit at floor level, where nothing else is competing for attention, and they run in five different directions at once. Your eye reads that as disorder even when the rest of the room is tidy.

The fix is not hiding every cable. It is making them all go the same way.

The principle: one path, not five

Before buying anything, look under your desk and count how many separate directions cables are travelling. On most desks it is four or five — one to the wall outlet, one down to a laptop on the floor, a couple hanging loose.

The goal is one. Everything gathers at a single point under the desk, then makes one trip down to the floor, then one trip to the wall.

You can do most of this before spending anything. The products below just make the tidy version stay tidy.

The power strip goes in the tray. All the transformer bricks go in the tray. The excess length of every cable gets coiled and goes in the tray. What was a pile on the floor becomes one object you cannot see from a standing position.

If you have a standing desk, this is not optional — the tray is what stops the cables from being yanked every time the desk changes height. Leave enough slack for the full range of travel and test it at the top before you tidy.

Step 2: give the cables one route down

With the tray in place, everything that still needs to reach the floor should follow one path, ideally down a single desk leg.

Step 3: deal with the cables you actually unplug

Charging cables are the ones that always end up on the floor, because they get moved several times a day and no clip survives that.

The trick is not to secure them but to weight them. A small clip at the desk edge that the cable end sits in, rather than passes through, means you drop it and it stays. Anything that requires threading gets abandoned by the third day.

What this looks like when it is done

From a seated position you should see the desk legs and the floor, and nothing else. From standing, nothing at all.

Total spend is low and most of the improvement comes from the tray alone. If you only do one thing on this list, do that one.

A note on what not to buy

Cable sleeves and braided wraps look impressive in product photos and are a mistake for most desks. They bundle cables into one thick tube, which is tidy until you need to change one cable — then you unwrap the whole thing. Trays and clips let you pull one cable out without touching the others.

Sleeves make sense for a run that never changes, like a desktop PC to a wall outlet. For a laptop desk, they cost you more time than they save.
